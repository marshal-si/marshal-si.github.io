The Problem

The most expensive code is written
before the problem is understood.

Most systems don't fail in production. They fail at the whiteboard — where an unexamined assumption becomes a requirement, and a requirement becomes a build.

Speculative engineering

Features and entire systems are built on assumptions nobody tested. The rework that follows costs far more than the question would have.

Opinion over evidence

Decisions driven by preference or seniority are hard to defend in a boardroom — and harder to unwind once they are embedded in a system.

Knowledge that walks out the door

When the reasoning behind a decision lives only in memory, every staff departure erodes the system, and every audit becomes archaeology.

Principles

Six rules the platform enforces — not just espouses.

Principles are easy to publish and easy to ignore. On Marshal they are built into the workflow itself: the system will not let work proceed past a checkpoint its documentation has not earned.

01Lineage & Knowledge before code

Raw operational signals are transformed into verified knowledge. Code is an implementation detail written only after the problem space is fully understood and mapped.

02Evidence before opinion

Every engineering decision is supported by evidence and recorded with its rationale — defensible in review, not just defensible in a meeting.

03Every decision is traceable

Ask “why was this built?” and the answer is a record — linked back through requirements to the evidence that justified them — not a recollection.

04Quality is engineered

Quality is designed into the process from the first step. Automated checks run before any review begins, and failures must be fixed — not waived.

05AI assists engineering

AI agents investigate, review, and draft at machine speed. Accountability, approval, and judgement remain with people.

06Every project improves the platform

Lessons from each engagement are captured and fed back. Knowledge compounds instead of resetting with every new project.

Why Marshal

Sometimes the right outcome of an investigation is the decision not to build.

A challenge that does not survive scrutiny closes with a written record of why — the evidence considered, the reasoning applied, and what was observed along the way. That record has real value: it stops budget being spent on the wrong thing, and it protects the next good idea from the same mistake. An honest “no”, evidenced, is worth more than an expensive “yes”.

Philosophy & Governance

AI as Elders and Chiefs. The Tribe holds the Wisdom.

In traditional governance, leadership is an intergenerational trust. Marshal applies this stewardship-driven model to software engineering: autonomous agents do the heavy lifting of the lower tiers, but governance and final judgment always rest with the community.
