The Problem

The most expensive code is written
before the problem is understood.

Most systems don't fail in production. They fail at the whiteboard — where an unexamined assumption becomes a requirement, and a requirement becomes a build.

Speculative engineering

Features and entire systems are built on assumptions nobody tested. The rework that follows costs far more than the question would have.

Opinion over evidence

Decisions driven by preference or seniority are hard to defend in a boardroom — and harder to unwind once they are embedded in a system.

Wisdom that dies with its keeper

When the reasoning behind a decision lives only in someone's memory, every departure erodes the system and every audit becomes archaeology. Marshal exists so that counsel is written down, kept, and handed on.

Governance Model

Every challenge is heard
before it is solved.

Marshal is run like a tribal gathering. The Tribe brings a challenge to the Chief, who convenes the Elders. The Elders consult the wisdom of the Ancestors to decide whether the challenge is real. Only a validated challenge becomes a problem, and only then does the collective work of solving it begin.

Lineage and understanding run in opposite directions. Wisdom is handed down from the Ancestors to the Tribe. Understanding climbs up from the Tribe's lived reality, through Data, Information and Knowledge, to Wisdom.

Rising: challenge to problem

The Tribe raises it. The Chief structures it. The Elders test it against the Ancestors' wisdom. It is validated as a problem, or closed with a written reason.

Descending: problem to solution

The Ancestors' precedent sets the rules. The Elders approve the approach. The Chief designs and builds. The Tribe tests and accepts.

Precedent guides; it does not bind

Elders may depart from the record, but only by writing down why. That record becomes the next ancestor.

Principles

Six rules the platform enforces — not just espouses.

Principles are easy to publish and easy to ignore. On Marshal they are built into the workflow itself: the system will not let work proceed past a checkpoint its documentation has not earned.

01Inherit before you invent

The Canon is consulted before any new work begins. Wisdom is handed down, and code is an implementation detail written only once the problem is understood and mapped.

02Validate before you solve

A challenge is not a problem until the Elders, guided by the Ancestors' record and the evidence, say it is. Preference and seniority are not evidence.

03Every decision has a lineage

Ask “why was this built?” and the answer is a record — linked back through requirements to the evidence and precedent that justified them — not a recollection.

04Quality is engineered

Quality is designed into the process from the first step. Automated checks run before any review begins, and failures must be fixed — not waived.

05Elders decide

AI agents investigate, review, and draft at machine speed. Accountable human domain experts approve, and answer for what they approve.

06Every project enriches the Canon

Lessons from each engagement are written back as precedent. Wisdom compounds instead of resetting with every new project.

Why Marshal

Sometimes the right outcome of an investigation is the decision not to build.

A challenge that does not survive scrutiny closes with a written record of why — the evidence considered, the reasoning applied, and what was observed along the way. That record has real value: it stops budget being spent on the wrong thing, and it protects the next good idea from the same mistake. An honest “no”, evidenced, is worth more than an expensive “yes”. A declined challenge is not a failure; it is precedent, handed on to whoever comes next.
